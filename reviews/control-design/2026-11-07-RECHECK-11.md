The remaining problems concern freshness and recovery.

  **Freshness judgment call: acceptable in principle, incomplete as written.** An authenticated “no update” answer tied to the held component version can count as confirmation, and preserving that time across restarts is right. But [CONTROL.md:548](/Users/pab/AresOS-council-control-layer/docs/design/CONTROL.md:548) also counts loading a new version as confirmation. Previously installed bytes, or an old image’s initial copy, can be newly loaded without being recently confirmed. Require evidence of recent download/confirmation; loading existing bytes must preserve their age, and missing evidence must mean unknown freshness. Brief 20 needs first-load and restored-state cases alongside ordinary restarts.

  **Recovery judgment call: not acceptable as stated.** [CONTROL.md:555](/Users/pab/AresOS-council-control-layer/docs/design/CONTROL.md:555) equates “no extension tag” with “not page traffic.” Yet [CONTROL.md:417](/Users/pab/AresOS-council-control-layer/docs/design/CONTROL.md:417) explicitly allows page traffic to lose those tags when the extension disappears. A fixed Google host list and full TLS authenticate the destination; matching the protocol’s shape does not establish the sender. Require trustworthy updater attribution, or verified tag coverage with recovery stopped or isolated when attribution is lost. Test genuine updates and matching page/worker requests during extension loss. Package signatures do not repair this distinction.

  **Version-selection judgment call: acceptable as a requirement.** Matching the data Chrome would actually use is the right contract at [CONTROL.md:536](/Users/pab/AresOS-council-control-layer/docs/design/CONTROL.md:536). Brief 20 must establish that equivalence, including compatibility and built-in floors; “highest complete” must remain subordinate to Chrome’s effective selection rules.
