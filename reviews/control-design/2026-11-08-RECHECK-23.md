Blocking findings remain.

- At [CONTROL.md:1550](/Users/pab/AresOS-council-control-layer/docs/design/CONTROL.md:1550), an executable allowlist does not establish the claimed containment of all attacker-controlled code. JavaScript loaded through require/import hooks, workers, eval, or an in-process feature needs no forbidden executable. Qualification must cover these routes in the pinned runtime, including sandbox-written files later interpreted by Claude Code. For a Bun-based binary, test runtime switches too: compiled executables can retain preload and interpreter modes. The fixed environment helps, but tracing program starts cannot settle this. I have not demonstrated an exploit against the pinned binary. [Bun documentation](https://bun.com/docs/runtime/code-generation-from-strings)

- The sandbox transition needs an explicit security contract. The wrapper enters a domain permitted to execute anything **before bubblewrap establishes confinement**. Require fixed restrictions that arguments cannot weaken, no arbitrary execution during setup, failure terminating the launch, and no transitions from sandbox workloads back into session, relay, hook or unconfined domains. Specify protection against relabelling and against executing or executable-mapping sandbox-written files. Explicitly qualify ptrace, memfd execution, loader invocation, and execmem/execmod. Any JIT exception must be acknowledged: executable-memory permission does not distinguish trusted from attacker-supplied JavaScript. These are separate SELinux controls, not consequences of an executable allowlist. [Kernel enforcement](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/master/security/selinux/hooks.c)

- The sign-in gate explicitly keeps service asking actions off. The general qualification gate is merely labelled “blocking.” Make **both successful gates, matching the installed binaries/settings/policy and active enforcement**, prerequisites for preparation and dispatch, including after restart. Missing, failed or stale qualification must leave them disabled. The log watcher also needs a closed failure policy: watcher death or lost audit events currently defeats the promised disabling after a refusal, although the kernel still blocks that execution.
