One blocking finding remains in [CONTROL.md:1568](/Users/pab/AresOS-council-control-layer/docs/design/CONTROL.md:1568): the group-and-parent check does not prove that requests came through Claude Code’s approval path.

A hostile, unsandboxed direct child of Claude Code can create pipes, fork a feeder process, attach those pipes to stdin/stdout, then execute the genuine setgid relay. Executing it preserves the registered parent while acquiring `aresos-relay`. The feeder supplies requests; both service checks pass without an approval. This is precisely a “missed route” that point (c) claims to contain. The attack follows from Linux’s [exec and descriptor-preservation rules](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/execve.2.html).

Ordinary Claude children inherit `aresos-session`, not `aresos-relay`; inheritance alone therefore does not impersonate the relay. But executing its setgid program can. A properly confined sandbox prevents that escalation; an unsandboxed direct child needs an independently enforced restriction. Register and authenticate the actual approved relay launch and input channel, or enforce a boundary preventing other children from acquiring that authority. Test this direct-child attack explicitly.

Non-dumpability addresses reopening the protected processes’ `/proc` descriptors and accessing their memory. It does **not** revoke already-held pipes or inherited descriptors. Require unrelated children to receive no MCP pipe ends or authenticated service sockets, and test inheritance: [socket peer credentials describe the connection’s establishment](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man7/unix.7.html), not whoever later holds its descriptor.

The second previous blocker is closed **at the design level**. Choice 5 precisely distinguishes review-first from accepting autonomous posting, deletion and sending to arbitrary service recipients. Section 7 explicitly applies (a) until the owner answers and refuses credentialed installation when review fails or cannot run. Add explicit tests for those refusal cases and the unanswered state.

Sandbox compatibility remains a qualification requirement: non-dumpability can interfere with [user-namespace mapping permissions](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man7/user_namespaces.7.html). Specify credential dropping before sandbox startup without weakening Claude or the relay. Setting `no_new_privs` on Claude itself would suppress the relay’s setgid transition.
- “Non-dumpable for life” also needs verification that neither pinned program re-enables dumpability; [setgid does not prohibit that syscall](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/PR_SET_DUMPABLE.2const.html).
