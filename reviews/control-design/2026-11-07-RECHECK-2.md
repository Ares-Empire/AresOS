**OWNER-DECISION — Free plain reading.** The reasoning holds as a usability tradeoff consistent with free navigation and typing; it is not a technical proof that every such request must be unrestricted for pages to load. Sections 6 and 10 now honestly disclose the encoded-traffic leak and who receives search queries, so I would retain the owner’s choice without counting it as a blocker. However, replace “only asking before typing would close that”: refusing input or restricting egress are other options, and approving typing accepts a disclosure risk rather than technically preventing hostile transmission; the headline “nothing Claude caused or entered leaves” must explicitly acknowledge this exception.

Anthropic’s current docs confirm the [SDK-hook exceptions](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/settings-reference#allowmanagedhooksonly), [in-process MCP exceptions](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/managed-mcp#exclusive-control-with-managed-mcp-json), and that [helpers and web tools run outside the shell sandbox](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sandboxing#what-runs-outside-the-sandbox). They also confirm that [file-tool restrictions do not constrain MCP](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permissions#block-file-tool-reads-and-writes-outside-the-working-directories), [Stop has interrupt/error exceptions and a continuation cap](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks#stop), and [explicit ask rules survive bypass mode](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permission-modes#actions-no-mode-auto-approves). The revised claims are sound within those limits.

Draft 3 introduces these additional consequences; the first two are counted under existing blockers, not counted twice:

- **Blocking, within #4 — Initiator ancestry misses shared-state causation.** Under the [new page-owned-activity exemption](../../docs/design/CONTROL.md:320), a timer created at page load can read a deletion queue populated by Claude’s later click and POST it: its callback ancestry still starts at page load, and no typed text exists. Require tracking of state-dependent effects, or conservatively hold unqualified non-reading traffic after relevant actions; add this exact fixture to 17c.

- **Blocking, within #8 — Restore recovery can reopen duplicate submission.** SMTP accepts an operation created after the backup, but the process crashes before saving Sent; after the owner overrides the older-log refusal, neither the restored log nor Sent contains evidence of that operation. Reject IDs from the invalidated generation or retain independent operation tombstones, and reconcile folder recovery’s “finished or undone” promise with §4.3’s prohibition on automatic resumption.

- **Should-fix — GraphQL classification needs explicit limits.** A read-only search query can still disclose private values in its variables; `query` versus `mutation` alone does not decide whether transmission needs approval. Make content and destination gates take precedence, and require qualification for ambiguous, persisted or batched operations; GraphQL’s [side-effect-free execution rule](https://spec.graphql.org/September2025/#sec-Normal-and-Serial-Execution) is not a confidentiality guarantee.

- **Should-fix — Same-address coalescing can discard distinct work.** Two unknown-background requests to `/graphql` or `/api` can contain different operations or records, yet [the newest-only rule](../../docs/design/CONTROL.md:338) discards one. Coalesce only adapter-proven equivalent read polling; otherwise retain separate operations and report any cancellation.

- **Should-fix — A separate Linux user does not provide separate managed policy.** The coding user still reads the machine’s managed settings: an assistant-wide no-network policy also blocks coding, while the fixed MCP configuration still starts proxies that cannot access its sockets. Keep the account separation, but define separate protected policy environments, such as containers with distinct managed configuration; [managed settings override ordinary user settings](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/settings#settings-precedence).
