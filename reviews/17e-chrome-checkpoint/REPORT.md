## Q1 — bypass

**run [C, `Q1_KERNEL_BYPASS`, route cases `ipv4`/`ipv6`/`localhost`/`linklocal`, `Q1_PROXY_DOWN` and `Q1_PROXY_DOWN_NAVIGATION`; L, `Q1_RESTART_BARRIER`, `Q1_RESTORED_FIRST_SCRIPT`]: PASS for the exercised paths.** Direct IPv4, IPv6, localhost and link-local receiver probes work with enforcement off and deliver zero requests with it on. These probes deliberately ignore the proxy while using Chrome's uid. Browser requests through all four routes work unguarded, have zero receiver headers/body before approval, and arrive once after approval. Redirects, page/worker traffic and the alternate transports below have positive controls. A down checkpoint gives a page failure and zero direct delivery ([native Chrome error](screenshots/core-proxy-down-navigation.png)). Persisted guards load before the proxy listens; the restored document is captured before extension attachment, and its first-script POST stays held after only its document GET is approved.

**read — [Chrome 157 proxy behavior](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/157.0.8103.38/net/docs/proxy.md) / assumed carry-over:** The root-owned policy selects one fixed proxy, no DIRECT fallback, and `<-loopback>`. An inet nftables output chain lets the dedicated browser uid connect only to `127.0.0.1:18080`; all other IPv4/IPv6/UDP output is rejected. This Linux mechanism is suitable for a Fedora launcher that always assigns Chrome and its helpers that uid. The actual Fedora/Wayland launcher, profile ownership and desktop integration are untested. A deployed launcher must also reserve/authenticate the listener and exclude unwrapped Chrome launches; hostile local port rebinding is not qualified by this IP/port test.

## What the owner would notice

**run / assumed consequence:** Ordinary anonymous reading mostly resembles direct Chrome, with the measured timing changes above. YouTube and Stack Overflow have robot gates, Reddit is blocked, and Notion never renders its document in either arm; this sample does not prove that interception never creates additional challenges. The site sees a different TLS/HTTP2 client. WebTransport and TURN-based data channels stop working. **run / assumed consequence:** A broad unresolved guard can stall unrelated tabs, background autosaves, polling and live messages, including reading requests. Without qualified adapters, eighteen small fixture operations mean eighteen decisions, not one notification per page. A lost capture must be attempted again as a new operation. An unknown dispatched result must be checked before retrying. **assumed owner decision:** Continuous local decryption exposes passwords, cookies and page contents to the checkpoint process; synthetic tests do not decide whether the owner accepts that trust boundary.

## Changes for the design and brief 20

**assumed recommendation, supported by [C, L, T]:** Replace draft 9 section 6's DNR floor and fail-then-repeat allowance with the external, continuously decrypting, OS-routed checkpoint. The checkpoint owns complete bytes, capture IDs, durable consumption and recovery state. Keep the extension for browser interaction, document/account signals, owner UI and supporting attribution. Keep wrappers/adapters for meaningful descriptions, causal hints, freezing and explicit cancellation; they must not become authorization or bypass the checkpoint. Retain guards across crashes and uncertain storage/handoffs; only qualified resolution or an informed owner decision can end them.

**assumed recommendation:** Brief 20 should build on a pinned, maintained mitmproxy core rather than a new TLS/HTTP2 implementation, carrying the receiver qualification forward. First close the core integration contracts: pre-write WS control frames, fail-stop addon errors, explicit HTTP/2 cancellation, account/document revalidation, service-network-context trust, bounded resource behavior and an always-enforced desktop launcher. Add qualified reading adapters before claiming low notification cost. Do not ship this diagnostic RPC, root-capable driver or fixture account model as product code.
