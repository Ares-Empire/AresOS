## View on the writer’s choices

I agree with dropping stale proposals immediately after a successful replay, including a restore-batch replay. SQLite applies the complete `set` and head atomically, then `_finish` appends the marker. A replay exception aborts startup. A second crash loses the in-memory queue and the next startup reevaluates the durable rows. A record-only tail stays locked. Comparing the **specific source key** yielded by `unfinished` also preserves partial and unknown outcomes and avoids confusion with unrelated cached legacy intents. The same-ID collision in finding 1 needs its own rule. `control/aresos_control/storage.py:235`, `control/aresos_control/storage.py:583`, `control/aresos_control/recovery.py:48`.

I agree with retaining passive all-app `pending` on an errored reply because the Stop hook consumes that section. The `tools` and `pending` methods belong on the read side. Cancellation, transfers, account setup, asking reads, and changes should retain their gate. After a fully undone first fence, the next independent healthy request can save the queue. A surviving latch continues to refuse writes until healthy restart and recovery. `control/aresos_control/hooks.py:132`, `control/aresos_control/requests.py:28`, `control/aresos_control/storage.py:782`.

The extra legacy caution, fresh-proxy startup refusal, and read-only Mail server checks are reasonable known limits. Qualify the restart wording: a generic `background-storage` notice can disappear without a durable source that recreates it. I see no new owner judgment call for preserving acknowledgements, reporting observed evidence accurately, or keeping the first refusal intact. T352 and T385 cover those decisions. `briefs/20-store-oracle-reads.md:432`, `control/aresos_control/engine.py:237`, `control/aresos_control/proxy.py:39`.
